A Field Guide to Night Photography

Photographing a city after sunset rewards deliberate preparation. Light levels change quickly, bright signs sit beside deep shadows, and a scene that looks quiet can become crowded during a long exposure. The best results come from making a few careful choices before opening the shutter.

Pack the essentials

  • A stable tripod
  • A small light
  • A clean lens cloth

Set the camera to a low sensitivity when the subject is still, then use the histogram to protect bright highlights. A cable release or short timer keeps a finger press from moving the camera. Review the corners of the frame, not only the center, because unwanted lamps often hide near an edge.

Expose for the light you want to preserve, not for the darkness surrounding it.

Finally, leave time to watch the scene without taking a picture. Traffic rhythms, reflections, and changing window lights often reveal a stronger composition than the first one you noticed. Patience is the most useful tool in the bag.
