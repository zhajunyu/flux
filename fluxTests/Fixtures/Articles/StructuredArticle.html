Visible Readability Headline

A mountain observatory has begun publishing a daily record of atmospheric clarity for schools and amateur astronomers. The program combines precise instruments with plain-language notes, making a difficult measurement useful to people planning an evening under the stars.

Each entry describes humidity, airborne dust, wind, and the stability of the upper atmosphere. Teachers can compare the readings with images taken by the observatory and ask students to explain why the sharpness of a planet changes from one night to the next.

The archive will also help researchers study slow seasonal patterns. A single clear night is memorable, but thousands of consistent observations can reveal how wildfire smoke, storms, and urban growth affect the view above the region.

Organizers designed the project as an open notebook rather than a polished annual report. They expect local observers to add corrections and photographs, creating a shared history of the sky that is both scientifically useful and easy to explore.
